# Sprint Backlog – Reflection

## Project: SafeStreet Community Security Solutions

During the development of SafeStreet, our team used Trello to organise sprint work into cards containing user stories and assigned tasks. This helped us break the project into manageable activities and understand what needed to be completed.

The sprint cards included activities related to understanding community security problems, gathering information from Khayelitsha residents and traders, and preparing findings to guide the development of the application. Each card contained a user story and subtasks assigned to team members.

Using Trello helped the team keep related tasks together and made responsibilities easier to identify. It also provided a central place to review the work planned for the project.

One important lesson I learned was that a sprint backlog needs to be clear and organised so that team members understand the work they are responsible for. Breaking larger activities into smaller tasks makes it easier to monitor progress and identify work that still needs attention.

A challenge in sprint planning is making sure that tasks are manageable, responsibilities are clear, and progress is monitored regularly. In future, I would improve our approach by ensuring that every sprint has a clearly documented goal, reviewing task statuses more frequently, and checking whether the planned work has been completed by the end of the sprint.

Overall, this activity helped me understand how a sprint backlog supports planning, teamwork, and progress tracking in Agile development. I learned that organising work into smaller tasks can help a team work more systematically towards its project goals.
