# User Stories and Tasks – Reflection

## Project: SafeStreet Community Security Solutions

During the development of the SafeStreet project, user stories helped our team understand the needs of the people who would use the application. Our project focuses on improving community safety in Khayelitsha by helping residents report security incidents and access information about security patrols.

We used the user story format to describe what users needed and why those features were important. For example, our team identified the need to interview Khayelitsha residents and traders to understand their security problems before developing the application. This helped us focus on real community needs rather than making assumptions.

We also broke user stories into smaller tasks and assigned responsibilities to team members using Trello. These tasks included recruiting interview participants, conducting interviews, analysing responses, preparing reports, and developing interview guides and consent forms. Breaking the work into smaller tasks made it easier to understand what needed to be done and who was responsible for each activity.

One thing I learned was that user stories help connect technical development with the needs of users. Task breakdown also helps team members organise their work and understand their responsibilities. Trello made it easier to keep the user stories and their related tasks together.

One challenge was ensuring that large activities were divided into manageable tasks and that responsibilities were clearly assigned. In future, I would improve our process by making acceptance criteria clearer for each user story and reviewing task progress regularly to identify delays earlier.

Overall, creating user stories and tasks helped me understand how Agile teams translate user needs into practical work. I learned that clear user stories, manageable tasks, and teamwork are important for developing a useful application such as SafeStreet.
